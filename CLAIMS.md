# Doorstub: public claims (draft)

Draft of 11/16. This file is the source for every public sentence about Doorstub: the site, the README, the deck and the video. Each claim names the rule that makes it true (see `SPEC.md`) and the tests that pin it. If a sentence is not here, do not publish it.

**Status today.** The contracts, the transaction builders, the attack suite, the sign-only wallet probe, the client libraries (chain, wallet, shows), a local test chain (simnet), the organiser's studio CLI, the worker (refund-notice signer, door, settle keeper; `worker/RUNTIME.md`) and the site (`app/`) exist, and the whole judge path runs end to end on simnet in a real browser with the real worker and keeper (`npm run e2e`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`), including a real show's walk-in raced by a front-runner who watches the mempool (limit 17). The screening room for the real shows (`app/room/`, sections 9.8 and 11, limits 19 to 24) is built, tested locally (`npm run test:room`, and `npm run e2e:room` in headless Chrome on a local stage) and deployed, for now with a 12-minute test film in place of the screening film; the worker also holds a room key (`ROOM_KEY`) that only makes room passes and the film's folder name (limit 24). Since 18 Nov 2026 Doorstub has run on Bitcoin Cash mainnet: the 55 shows (52 sample A instances, sample B and the two real shows, the online screenings of section 6) were created there, with every genesis confirmed at block 977919 (`app/public/shows.json`); the site runs at https://doorstub.pages.dev with the refund-notice signer, the door (its record in the `DOOR_CLAIMS` KV namespace) and the screening room; and the keeper runs every 5 minutes as a cron Worker with the samples' data key. A claim marked *(not built yet)* or *(not deployed yet)* must not be published in the present tense until it ships.

## 1. Headline

> Tickets that refund themselves. The organiser is paid for each guest who walks in. Every other ticket refunds.

It is true as written because of three contract rules:

1. The organiser receives a ticket's price only through `checkIn`, and only the ticket holder's own wallet can sign a check-in (`SPEC.md` 3.2).
2. After the show plus a grace period, `settle` pays every coin that was not checked in back to its buyer. Anyone can send that transaction; it needs no key.
3. There is no "organiser collects after the start" path. A show that never happens has no check-ins, so every ticket refunds.

Small print that must sit next to the headline wherever there is room:

- "Refunds return the same BCH, minus a network fee of at most N sats and any fee the organiser posted on the show page." (N is the show's `minerFeeCap`, 1,200 to 2,000 sats; the samples use 1,500.)
- "Someone has to send the refund transaction. Anyone can, without a key, and Doorstub's keeper does it for you." *(Keeper built and tested on simnet and in local workerd, `test/worker/keeper.test.js` and `npm run worker:workerd-check`; deployed on 18 Nov 2026 as a cron Worker every 5 minutes, with the samples' data key.)*

## 3. Words we do not use

- **"First", "new", "the only", "never been done".** False. See section 5.
- **"Before the show."** BCH cannot enforce a deadline. Say "until you walk in or the show settles".
- **"Full refund"** on its own. Always add "minus the network fee and any posted fee".
- **Dollar amounts as a promise.** Prices are in BCH. A dollar figure is today's rate only.
- **"Your seat goes back on sale"** after a hand-back. False: the seat counter only grows.
- **"Automatic"** without saying who sends the transaction.
- **"Trustless entry"** or anything that promises you will get in. The contract pays the organiser when you check in; whether the door then lets you in is between you and the organiser.

## 4. Limits we state

These go on the show page, in the README and in the deck, in plain words.

1. **Prices are in BCH.** A refund returns the same BCH, not the same dollars.
2. **"Before the show" cannot be enforced.** Hand-back and check-in work for as long as the ticket's money is still in the contract, even after the start. After `startHeight + graceBlocks`, whoever moves first (the holder, or anyone settling) decides the path.
3. **Hand-backs do not free a seat.** v1 shows have no seat cap, so this cannot lock a show up.
4. **The organiser key can refund money but never redirect it.** A per-seat (`'R'`) or whole-show (`'C'`) notice sends money back to buyers only. Up to N sats of each ticket may go to the network fee; whoever sends the refund decides how much of that allowance to use, and nobody can keep it. A `'C'` notice also refunds seats sold after it was signed.
5. **A ticket moved away from the buyer's address** can no longer check in or hand back. Settle still refunds the original buyer, so a gifted ticket's refund goes to the giver.
6. **The network-fee allowance can be burned, not kept.** Whoever sends a settle or refund (anyone), and the holder in a hand-back or check-in, sets the network fee, up to `minerFeeCap` of each ticket. No path allows an output that could take the rest, so a griefer can burn the allowance but cannot pocket it; a sender who mines the block, or gets a rebate from a miner, could collect it. The app accepts a cap of 1,200 to 2,000 sats and at most 20% of the price (a check-in, the largest transaction, is about 1,066 bytes); the samples use 1,500. The show page must state the cap.
7. **Posted fees.** The organiser may post a hand-back fee and a no-show fee, in basis points of the coin's value (0 on the samples). Fees under 546 sats are waived; a fee that is due is paid exactly. The app refuses a show with either fee over 10% (1,000 bps). The contract does not range-check them (a show deployed by hand could post more), so the app must show both fees.
8. **Check-in is final.** Once you check in, the organiser has the money.
9. **Buyers need BCH already.** There is no card or cash door.
10. **The app must verify the show's genesis transaction** (`verifyGenesis`), taken from its own node rather than the show page and confirmed on chain. The box office only sells tickets of the show's own token, so no genesis and no stranger can lock or take a buyer's money; a malformed genesis can still repeat seat numbers, so the app refuses a show whose genesis fails the check, or whose fees or network-fee cap are outside the bounds in 6 and 7.
11. **Your wallet must sign with `SIGHASH_ALL`** (0x41 or 0x61). The app must check every wallet signature and refuse anything else (the probe already does). That only helps because the app asks the wallet not to broadcast and sends the checked transaction itself; the probe never broadcasts at all.
12. **A check-in is a transaction of its own** (your ticket and its money in, the payout and your keepsake out), and two buyers on the same box-office shard at once means one of them signs again.
13. **Coins sent straight to a contract address without the right token are stuck for good.** Only use the app.
14. **Sales end in the app, not on chain.** BCH cannot stop a sale after the start. The app refuses to sell at or after the start or after a cancel (and a real show's tickets once its event ends, by the reader's clock), checking again right before it sends a buy the wallet approved. Only the organiser closes a started show's box-office shards, by hand with the studio's `close`, which needs the sales key; the keeper does not hold it. Until then the shards stay open on chain, and the site stops selling at the start block. Someone who buys late with other software can be settled at once and loses the no-show fee plus up to N sats of network fee (up to N sats after a cancel).
15. **Batches.** Anyone can settle or refund up to 100 tickets per transaction; a bigger show takes several. A buy takes at most 50 of your coins.
16. **Network fees are paid from each ticket's allowance.** Hand-back, check-in, settle and refund transactions cannot take a coin from anyone else to pay the network fee. If network fees ever rose above about 1.1 sat per byte (the exact level depends on the show's cap), those transactions would wait until fees fell.
17. **The event link of a real show is off chain.** The contract pays the organiser when your wallet checks in; the link to the event comes from Doorstub's door. Before your page sends the check-in, it reserves that check-in at the door with a secret it keeps. After sending, it asks with the same secret, and the door gives the link once it has read your check-in from the network. Someone who sees your check-in in the mempool and asks the door gets nothing: one claimant per check-in (`SPEC.md` section 12; `test/worker/door.test.js` with an observer that reads the mempool, and `npm run e2e` with a front-runner that polls the show's Escrow mempool and asks before the page does). The reservation is kept 120 days, so it does not lapse while the door cannot answer yet. The limits: this protection depends on the page's one reservation try, which is not fatal: if it fails, the page still sends the check-in, and the door then gives the link to the first caller. The free Cloudflare KV plan caps writes at 1,000 a day and a reservation needs no ticket, so someone with many IPs could use them up; the door's record then falls back to each Cloudflare isolate's memory for the rest of that day (a paid plan bills writes instead; `worker/RUNTIME.md` section 5). Also, the door's record is in Cloudflare KV, which is eventually consistent, so two Cloudflare locations can disagree for a short time. The door reads one Fulcrum server and accepts a check-in that is not confirmed yet, so a holder who races a conflicting hand-back past it, or a dishonest server in the pinned list, could get the link without the organiser being paid. Only the link leaks that way; no money moves. Whether the event then lets you in is between you and the organiser (section 3, "Trustless entry").
18. **What the page reads from the chain is one server's word.** The page checks every transaction it fetches against its txid, but confirmations, address history and the proof-strip counts come from whichever pinned Fulcrum server answers, with no merkle proof. A dishonest server could show a made-up history or call a genesis confirmed when it is not. It cannot move money: every transaction the page sends is checked in the 2026 VM first, and the network runs the scripts again.
19. **The screening room's film is unlisted, not gated.** The film's files sit on the site in one folder whose name is made from `ROOM_KEY` (`/room/media/<folder>/`, `worker/lib/room.js` `roomMediaFolder`). The room page learns the folder from the room link, and the site serves those files to anyone who asks for them, so anyone who has one room link can share the film.
20. **A room link is a bearer link.** It carries a pass for one seat's check-in: the first 32 hex of an HMAC under `ROOM_KEY` over the show, the seat label and the check-in txid (`worker/lib/room.js` `roomPass`). Anyone given the link enters that room, on any device. The pass is the same every time the door gives the link, and one seat's pass cannot be revoked; only a different `ROOM_KEY` would end every room link at once. The link opens the film and the chat; it cannot move money.
21. **The room's answers are picked from the written FAQ by matching words, in the page.** The page compares the question's words with each written answer's questions and keywords (`worker/lib/room-match.js`) and shows the closest written answer (or two close ones) from section 11, a fixed refusal or the fallback. A question never leaves the browser; the page keeps it only in its own transcript until the page closes. Matching words can pick an answer that does not fit the question, and nothing answers what the written FAQ does not cover. An AI model that would only pick among the matcher's candidates is planned and is not built.
22. **Questions close 10 minutes after the event ends.** The page takes questions until `eventAt` + `durationMin` + 10 min, on the server's clock as the page measures it, then says the screening has ended. "Watch from the start" stays for 4 days after that; then the room offers no film, though a play already running is not stopped. The files stay on the site (limit 19) until a deploy without them.
23. **The room needs JavaScript.** Without it the page shows only its no-JavaScript line (section 9.8). It plays the film with hls.js, or with the browser's own HLS player on Apple WebKit (Safari and every iOS browser). It has been tested in headless Chrome with hls.js only, not yet in Safari or on an iPhone.
24. **A leaked `ROOM_KEY` lets its holder make room links.** The worker holds `ROOM_KEY` as a Pages secret, to check room passes and to fill the room links the door gives (`worker/RUNTIME.md` section 4). Its holder could make a room link for any seat of any show and find the film's folder. It moves no money: it is not a wallet, sales or data key, and no contract knows it.

## 5. Prior art

Doorstub is not a new idea, and we say so. These projects did it first or did parts of it, and we credit them by name:

- **Turnout** (Arc, mainnet according to its README): dollar tickets held in escrow and released to the host per ticket at the door, holder-signed check-in, refunds for cancelled events and unscanned tickets.
- **Firstrow** (Monad testnet, Monad Metropolis 2026): money held in escrow until the fan walks in; fans refund themselves.
- **Curtain** (Monad testnet, Monad Metropolis 2026): pay-on-entry ticket escrow; an on-chain check-in pays the organiser; refunds without anyone approving them.
- **TixPact** (NEAR testnet, NEAR Hackathon 2026): event tickets whose refund does not depend on the seller.
- **FundMe and its CashStarter contracts** (Bitcoin Cash): refund a pledge by burning its receipt NFT, with refunds still open after a creator cancels. Doorstub's hand-back burns the ticket the same way.
- **Dance.cash** (a 2025 Bitcoin Cash hackathon entry): class tickets sold as NFTs on Bitcoin Cash.
- **The ShowUp spec** (Bitcoin Cash, published as a concept and withdrawn before BCH BLAZE 2026): deposits held in a covenant, released at check-in, refunded by default.
- **Waybill** (a BCH BLAZE 2026 entry): a similar release-at-the-door covenant for parcels; the seller is paid when the buyer's code is scanned at handoff, with a refund after a deadline.

What Doorstub adds on Bitcoin Cash, stated modestly: one escrow coin per ticket identified by a marker NFT, a check-in that the holder's own wallet signs and that leaves the ticket as a keepsake, a no-show settle that anyone can push, per-seat and whole-show refund notices, and parallel box-office shards so many buyers can buy at once.

For talks and films only, the same point in one positive line: "Doorstub builds on earlier work, and names it." Use it only where the projects above are then named, as the film's prior-art shot does. It is true because these projects did it first or did parts of it, Doorstub's hand-back burns the ticket the way FundMe's refunds burn a pledge receipt, and every one is credited by name here, in the README and on the site. "Earlier work" means these projects and the libraries credited in section 9.6, never earlier Doorstub code: Doorstub was built from scratch during BCH BLAZE 2026 (section 9.1). The site keeps its own sentence ("Doorstub is not a new idea, and we say so.", section 9.6).
